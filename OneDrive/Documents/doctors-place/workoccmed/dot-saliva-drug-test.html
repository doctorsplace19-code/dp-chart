DOT Saliva Drug Testing:
What Is and Is Not Allowed

Many drivers and employers ask whether an oral fluid (saliva) drug test counts for DOT compliance. The short answer: not yet. Here is exactly what the regulations say, when saliva tests are used, and what a valid DOT drug test requires.

The Bottom Line

Does DOT Allow Saliva Drug Testing?

❌

Saliva tests do NOT satisfy DOT drug testing requirements

Under 49 CFR Part 40, all DOT-mandated drug tests must use a urine specimen collected at a DOT-approved site and analyzed at a SAMHSA-certified laboratory. A saliva or oral fluid test result cannot be submitted as a valid DOT drug test, regardless of the brand, sensitivity, or lab that processes it.

This applies to every DOT-regulated employer and every type of DOT drug test: pre-employment, random, post-accident, reasonable suspicion, return-to-duty, and follow-up testing.

If a driver takes a saliva test and submits it as their DOT pre-employment drug test, the employer has not met the federal requirement. The driver cannot legally begin safety-sensitive functions until a proper urine-based DOT test is completed.

Why Urine Only?

The Science and Regulation Behind the Rule

49 CFR Part 40 specifies urine as the required specimen type because it has the longest detection window for most drugs (typically 1–3 days for single use, longer for heavy use), is the most thoroughly researched specimen type in forensic drug testing, and has an established chain of custody process that has been validated in federal courts.

Detection window comparison

Test Type Detection Window DOT Compliant? Typical Use
Urine 1–3 days (up to 30 days for heavy cannabis use) Yes All DOT testing
Oral Fluid (Saliva) Minutes to 24–48 hours Not yet Non-DOT, roadside impairment screening
Hair Up to 90 days No Non-DOT, background checks
Blood Hours No Post-accident impairment (clinical)
Breath Alcohol Minutes to hours Yes (alcohol only) DOT alcohol testing via EBT

Note: Saliva tests detect very recent use. For roadside impairment screening by law enforcement, oral fluid can show same-hour use that a urine test might miss. This is why some state police use it — but it serves a different purpose than the compliance testing DOT requires.

The Future

Is Oral Fluid DOT Testing Coming?

Yes — eventually. FMCSA published a final rule in May 2023 adding oral fluid as an alternative specimen type under 49 CFR Part 40. However, SAMHSA (the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration) must first certify qualifying oral fluid laboratories before employers can use this option.

⏳

Not yet available as of 2025

As of 2025, no SAMHSA-certified oral fluid laboratories had been approved. Until at least two labs are certified, oral fluid cannot be used for DOT testing. Monitor SAMHSA announcements for updates. Until then, urine is the only valid DOT specimen type.

When oral fluid testing does become available, employers will have the choice — not the requirement — to use it as an alternative. Urine testing will remain valid and will not be replaced.

What DOT Tests Screen For

The 5-Panel DOT Drug Test

All DOT urine drug tests screen for the same five drug categories, regardless of which DOT agency (FMCSA, FAA, FRA, FTA, PHMSA, or USCG) requires the test.

1

Marijuana (THC)

THC metabolite (THCA). Federally illegal regardless of state law.

2

Cocaine

Benzoylecgonine metabolite.

3

Opioids

Codeine, morphine, heroin, oxycodone, oxymorphone, hydrocodone, hydromorphone.

4

Amphetamines

Amphetamine, methamphetamine, MDMA, MDA.

5

PCP

Phencyclidine.

ℹ️

Medical marijuana does not exempt you from DOT testing

DOT regulations follow federal law. A state medical marijuana card or prescription does not exempt a CDL driver from DOT drug testing requirements. A positive THC result is a violation regardless of the driver's medical authorization.

How DOT Testing Works

What to Expect at a DOT Drug Screen

1

Order your test or receive a referral

Employers order through a C/TPA or directly. Drivers can also self-order a pre-employment screen through portal.dot-physical.net.

2

Go to a collection site

Walk in to any of 15,000+ certified collection sites. Bring a valid photo ID.

3

Provide a urine specimen

The collector follows DOT chain-of-custody procedures. The specimen is split into two bottles (A and B) and sealed in your presence.

4

Lab analysis at a SAMHSA-certified lab

The specimen is shipped to a certified laboratory for analysis. Typical turnaround is 24–48 hours.

5

MRO review and result

A Medical Review Officer (MRO) reviews the lab result, contacts the donor if needed, and reports the verified result to the employer or C/TPA.
