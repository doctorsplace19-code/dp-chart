Non-CDL DOT Physical: FRA, FAA, FTA & PHMSA

DOT physicals are not just for truck drivers. Railroad engineers, transit operators, pipeline workers, and aviation mechanics each have their own medical requirements — often very different from FMCSA. Here is how to find out which exam you actually need.

MD
Medically reviewed by Chantal Gabriel, MD  ·  Updated September 2026

When most people say "DOT physical," they mean the FMCSA commercial driver medical examination — the exam CDL drivers need to get their medical card. But the Department of Transportation has five operating agencies, each with their own safety-sensitive workforce and their own medical standards. If you work in railroad, transit, pipeline, or aviation, you may need a completely different kind of DOT exam — or no exam at all, just drug testing.

The Five DOT Agencies and Who They Cover

FMCSA

Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration
  • CDL holders operating CMVs in interstate commerce
  • Non-CDL drivers operating vehicles over 10,001 lbs
  • Drivers transporting placarded hazmat
  • Drivers carrying 9+ passengers for compensation
  • Exam: DOT physical / CMV medical certificate
  • Examiner: FMCSA Certified Medical Examiner (CME)
  • Frequency: Up to 2 years

FRA

Federal Railroad Administration
  • Locomotive engineers (49 CFR Part 240)
  • Conductors (49 CFR Part 242)
  • Other operating crew members
  • Exam: FRA medical examination
  • Examiner: Licensed physician familiar with FRA standards
  • Frequency: Every 3 years (annually for some conditions)

FTA

Federal Transit Administration
  • Bus operators, train operators
  • Revenue vehicle maintenance personnel
  • Security personnel with access to revenue vehicles
  • Medical exam: Employer/state-specific (no federal FTA medical card)
  • Drug & alcohol: Required under 49 CFR Part 655
  • Frequency: Per employer policy or state law

PHMSA

Pipeline & Hazardous Materials Safety Administration
  • Pipeline operators (operators, controllers, maintenance)
  • Hazmat workers (certain functions)
  • Medical exam: No standardized PHMSA medical certificate
  • Drug & alcohol: Required under 49 CFR Part 199
  • Fitness: Employer policy and state law govern

FAA

Federal Aviation Administration
  • Pilots (all classes)
  • Air traffic controllers
  • Flight engineers
  • Exam: FAA First, Second, or Third Class medical
  • Examiner: FAA Aviation Medical Examiner (AME) only
  • Frequency: 6 months to 5 years depending on class/age

FMCSA Non-CDL DOT Physical

This is the most common "non-CDL DOT physical" question — drivers who operate vehicles that require FMCSA medical certification but do not require a Commercial Driver's License. These include:

  • Drivers of straight trucks or box trucks over 10,001 lbs in interstate commerce
  • Drivers transporting placarded hazardous materials regardless of vehicle size
  • Drivers of vehicles carrying 9–15 passengers for direct compensation
  • Drivers of vehicles carrying 16 or more passengers regardless of compensation

These drivers need the same FMCSA DOT physical as CDL holders — performed by a Certified Medical Examiner listed on the National Registry — but they may not need a CDL if they operate only in intrastate commerce or below the CDL weight threshold. The resulting medical certificate is identical: the MCSA-5876 card.

The FMCSA physical and the CDL are separate requirements. You can have a valid FMCSA medical certificate without a CDL (if your vehicle does not legally require one), and you can have a CDL that is downgraded to non-CMV status if your medical certificate lapses. They are linked but distinct.

FRA Railroad Physical

Locomotive engineers and conductors are regulated by the FRA under 49 CFR Part 240 and Part 242. The medical standards differ from FMCSA in important ways:

FTA Transit Worker Drug Testing

The FTA does not issue a medical card or require a standardized physical for transit workers. Instead, FTA-regulated employers must have a drug and alcohol testing program under 49 CFR Part 655 for safety-sensitive employees, which includes:

  • Pre-employment drug testing
  • Random drug and alcohol testing (50% drug / 25% alcohol minimum rates)
  • Post-accident testing
  • Reasonable suspicion testing
  • Return-to-duty and follow-up testing after violations

Many transit agencies also require their own fitness-for-duty examinations under state law or employer policy, but these are not federally standardized the way FMCSA or FRA exams are.

PHMSA Pipeline Worker Requirements

Pipeline operators must have a drug and alcohol testing program under 49 CFR Part 199 for "covered employees" — those who perform operations, maintenance, or emergency response on gas or hazardous liquid pipelines. The testing requirements parallel FMCSA: pre-employment, random, post-accident, reasonable suspicion, RTD, and follow-up.

PHMSA does not require a standardized medical certificate. Fitness-for-duty standards are set by the pipeline operator and may be governed by state occupational health laws, OSHA standards, or collective bargaining agreements.

FAA Aviation Medical

FAA medical certificates are entirely separate from DOT occupational medicine and must be issued by an FAA-designated Aviation Medical Examiner (AME). There are three classes:

  • First Class — required for airline transport pilots. Most stringent standards. Valid 6 months for pilots over 40, 12 months for under 40.
  • Second Class — required for commercial pilots. Valid 12 months.
  • Third Class — required for private pilots. Valid 24–60 months depending on age.

WorkOccMed physicians are not FAA Aviation Medical Examiners and do not perform FAA medical examinations. FAA physicals must be scheduled through an FAA-designated AME.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a non-CDL DOT physical?

A non-CDL DOT physical is either (1) an FMCSA medical examination for a driver who operates a CMV that requires medical certification but not a CDL, or (2) a medical examination required by another DOT agency — FRA, FTA, FAA, or PHMSA — for safety-sensitive workers in those industries. The term is used differently in different contexts.

Do you need a CDL for a DOT physical?

No. CDL drivers need an FMCSA DOT physical, but so do some non-CDL CMV operators. Workers in railroad, transit, pipeline, and aviation may also need DOT agency medical clearances that have nothing to do with a CDL.

Who needs an FMCSA DOT physical vs. another DOT agency physical?

FMCSA physicals are for CMV drivers — truck and bus operators in interstate commerce. FRA physicals are for railroad engineers and conductors. FTA drug testing covers transit workers. PHMSA drug testing covers pipeline operators. FAA physicals are for pilots and air traffic controllers. Your job title and employer determine which agency's rules apply to you.

Can any doctor do a non-CDL DOT physical?

For FMCSA exams (including non-CDL CMV operators), only a Certified Medical Examiner on the National Registry can issue the medical certificate. FRA exams must be performed by a licensed physician familiar with FRA standards. FAA physicals require an FAA-designated Aviation Medical Examiner. FTA and PHMSA do not specify a single examiner credential for their medical clearance requirements.

Does WorkOccMed perform non-CDL DOT physicals?

WorkOccMed performs FMCSA medical examinations through our network of Certified Medical Examiners — for both CDL drivers and non-CDL CMV operators. For FRA railroad physicals and FTA drug testing, contact us to discuss your specific requirement. We do not perform FAA Aviation Medical Examinations.
