DOT SAP Evaluation Process: What to Expect

After a DOT drug or alcohol violation, a Substance Abuse Professional (SAP) evaluation is required by federal law before you can return to any safety-sensitive duties. Here is what the SAP evaluation involves and what happens at each step.

Medically reviewed by Chantal Gabriel, MD Updated September 2026

You cannot drive until the SAP process is complete. No exceptions — no safety-sensitive functions of any kind until you complete the SAP evaluation, prescribed treatment, and pass a return-to-duty drug test.

What Is a DOT SAP?

A Substance Abuse Professional (SAP) is a licensed or certified physician, psychologist, social worker, EAP professional, or drug and alcohol counselor with DOT-specific training. The SAP's role is to evaluate the driver after a violation and prescribe education or treatment — not to provide the treatment themselves.

You are responsible for finding your own SAP. Your consortium, employer assistance program, or C/TPA can often provide a referral. SAPs may offer in-person or telehealth evaluations depending on your state.

What Happens at the SAP Evaluation

The SAP conducts a face-to-face clinical interview lasting 1–3 hours. It covers:

  • The violation — what happened, which test type, what was found
  • Substance use history — how long, how often, what substances
  • Prior treatment — any prior counseling, treatment, or AA/NA participation
  • Mental health history — co-occurring conditions that may affect the recommendation

Based on this evaluation, the SAP prescribes one of several levels of care: a substance use education program, outpatient counseling, intensive outpatient (IOP), or residential treatment. The SAP's clinical judgment drives the prescription — it is not negotiable and cannot be appealed by seeking a second opinion.

Follow-Up Testing After Returning to Duty

After passing your RTD drug test, the SAP prescribes a follow-up testing schedule. Federal minimum: 6 unannounced tests in the first 12 months. The SAP can extend follow-up testing up to 60 months based on clinical judgment. Follow-up tests are separate from your regular random pool testing — you will receive both. Your consortium or C/TPA coordinates the follow-up schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who must complete a SAP evaluation?

Any safety-sensitive employee — CDL drivers, airline crew, transit operators, railroad workers — who has a DOT drug or alcohol violation must complete a SAP evaluation before returning to duty. This includes a verified positive drug test, alcohol test of 0.04+, refusal to test, reasonable suspicion violation, or a post-accident violation.

Is the SAP evaluation confidential?

Yes. The SAP's clinical findings — your history, diagnosis, and what was discussed — are confidential medical information. The SAP communicates to the employer only whether you have been evaluated, whether you have complied with treatment, and when you are eligible for the RTD test. The employer cannot see clinical notes or treatment details without your separate consent.

Can I get a second opinion on the SAP's prescription?

No. DOT regulations require that you follow the first SAP's recommended treatment program. You cannot see a different SAP to seek a less intensive recommendation — the first SAP's prescription stands. The only recourse is to challenge the SAP's qualifications or process, which is a very high bar.

Does the SAP violation go on my permanent record?

The underlying violation is reported to the FMCSA Clearinghouse and stays there for 5 years. Prospective employers who query you in the Clearinghouse will see the violation, the RTD process completion, and the current follow-up testing status.

What if I cannot find or afford a SAP?

There is no exemption for cost or availability — you must complete the SAP process before returning to duty. SAMHSA's national helpline (1-800-662-4357) can refer you to low-cost treatment options. Many states have employee assistance programs. Your consortium or C/TPA can also provide SAP referrals. Delaying the SAP only extends the time you are out of duty.
