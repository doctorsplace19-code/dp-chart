Return-to-Duty Drug Test

The RTD drug test is directly observed, SAP-authorized, and must come back negative before you can drive again. Here is exactly how it works, what it tests for, and what happens if you fail.

You cannot order your own RTD test. The test must be employer-directed following written authorization from your SAP. If you take a drug test on your own before SAP clearance, it does not count as the RTD test and your prohibited status will not be lifted.

What Makes the RTD Drug Test Different

The DOT return-to-duty drug test looks identical to a regular DOT 5-panel urine test on paper — same panel, same SAMHSA-certified lab, same MRO review. The critical differences are:

  • Direct observation is mandatory. Under 49 CFR 40.67, a same-sex observer must watch the specimen leave your body into the collection cup. This is not a standard requirement for random or pre-employment tests (unless there is a specific reason to observe). For RTD, it is always required.
  • SAP authorization is required. Your SAP must provide written documentation to your employer that you have completed the prescribed education or treatment and are ready for the RTD test. The test cannot happen without this step.
  • The Clearinghouse updates on result. The MRO reports the verified negative result directly to the FMCSA Clearinghouse, which lifts your prohibited status. This is the specific event that allows you to return to safety-sensitive work.
  • Failure resets the SAP process. A positive RTD test requires returning to the SAP for re-evaluation — you do not simply re-test.

What the RTD Drug Test Screens For

The DOT return-to-duty drug test uses the standard 5-panel, regardless of what substance caused the original violation:

Marijuana
THC metabolites (THCA)
Cocaine
Benzoylecgonine
Opioids
Codeine, morphine, heroin, oxycodone, hydrocodone
Amphetamines
Amphetamine, methamphetamine, MDMA
PCP
Phencyclidine

Even if your original violation was for marijuana, the RTD test screens all five panels. A positive result on any panel — even a substance unrelated to your original violation — counts as a failure and resets the process.

The RTD Test Process, Step by Step

1

SAP Clears You for Testing

Your SAP determines you have completed the prescribed education or treatment program and provides written authorization to your employer. Without this, no RTD test can take place.

2

Employer Coordinates the Test

Your employer (through their C/TPA or testing provider) selects a certified collection site capable of directly observed collections and sends you an authorization. The test is unannounced — you go immediately.

3

Directly Observed Collection

At the collection site, a same-sex observer accompanies you and watches the specimen leave your body into the collection cup. The collector documents the observed collection on the chain-of-custody form. You cannot refuse observation — refusal is treated as a positive test.

4

Lab Analysis and MRO Review

The specimen goes to a SAMHSA-certified laboratory. Negative results are reported within 24–72 hours. Non-negative specimens undergo GC/MS confirmation, and the MRO contacts you to review any legitimate medical explanation before reporting the final result.

5

Result Reported to Clearinghouse

The MRO reports the verified negative result to your employer and to the FMCSA Clearinghouse. Your prohibited status is lifted. Your employer can confirm this in the Clearinghouse before returning you to duty.

6

Follow-Up Testing Begins

You return to safety-sensitive duties, but follow-up testing starts immediately. The SAP's plan — a minimum of 6 unannounced directly observed tests in 12 months — is now active in addition to your regular random pool testing.

What Happens If You Fail the RTD Test

A positive RTD test is a second violation — and it is reported to the FMCSA Clearinghouse immediately. You remain in prohibited status and cannot drive. The process resets:

  1. The MRO reports the positive result to your employer and the Clearinghouse
  2. You must return to your SAP for re-evaluation
  3. The SAP prescribes additional education or treatment (typically more intensive than the first prescription)
  4. You complete the new prescription
  5. The SAP authorizes another RTD test — again directly observed

There is no limit on attempts, but each failure makes the next prescription more intensive. SAPs are required to escalate treatment recommendations for repeated violations. A second positive RTD test typically results in inpatient treatment requirements rather than outpatient education.

RTD Alcohol Test

If your original violation was an alcohol test result of 0.04 BAC or higher, the return-to-duty test is a breath alcohol test — not a urine drug test. The passing threshold is below 0.02 BAC. Direct observation applies to both types of RTD tests.

If you had both a drug violation and an alcohol violation, the SAP may authorize both an RTD drug test and an RTD alcohol test before clearing you to return to duty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the return-to-duty drug test directly observed?

Yes, always. Direct observation is mandatory for all DOT return-to-duty drug tests under 49 CFR 40.67. A same-sex observer must watch the specimen leave your body. Refusal to be observed is treated as a refusal to test — which is the same as a positive result.

Who authorizes the return-to-duty drug test?

Your SAP authorizes it after you complete the prescribed education or treatment. Your employer then coordinates the test. You cannot self-order an RTD test — a self-ordered test does not lift your prohibited status.

What happens if you fail the return-to-duty drug test?

You remain prohibited. The positive result is reported to the Clearinghouse as a second violation. You must return to your SAP for re-evaluation, complete a new (typically more intensive) prescription, and then undergo another directly observed RTD test.

Can I drive immediately after passing the return-to-duty test?

Yes — once the MRO reports the negative result to the Clearinghouse and your status updates to "not prohibited," your employer can return you to safety-sensitive duties. The Clearinghouse update usually happens within 1–2 business days of the MRO result.

How long does the return-to-duty drug test take to get results?

A negative result is typically reported within 24–72 hours of collection. Non-negative results take longer — GC/MS confirmation plus MRO interview can add 3–7 business days.

What is the follow-up testing plan after passing?

The SAP creates a follow-up testing plan: minimum 6 unannounced, directly observed tests in the first 12 months after returning to duty, with the option to extend up to 5 years. Follow-up tests are in addition to your regular random pool testing.

Is there an RTD test for alcohol violations?

Yes. If the original violation was an alcohol test of 0.04 BAC or higher, the RTD test is a breath alcohol test with a result below 0.02. Drug violations require a urine RTD drug test. Both types are directly observed.
