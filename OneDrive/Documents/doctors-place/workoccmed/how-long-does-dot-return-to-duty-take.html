How Long Does DOT Return to Duty Take?

There is no fixed date. The timeline depends mostly on your SAP and on how fast you complete each step. Here is what controls it.

Medically reviewed by Chantal Gabriel, MD Updated October 2026

Drivers always ask how long return to duty will take. The honest answer is that it depends on you and your SAP, but the order of events is fixed, and knowing it lets you plan.

Short answer: there is no set deadline. You return to safety-sensitive duty only after the SAP clears you, you complete the recommended education or treatment, and you pass the return-to-duty test with a negative result.

What controls the timeline

  1. Getting the SAP evaluation. Contact a SAP as soon as you can. Waiting here is the most common delay.
  2. Education or treatment. The SAP decides what you need and how long it takes. This part varies the most.
  3. The SAP follow-up evaluation. The SAP confirms you completed the recommendations and are eligible for the return-to-duty test.
  4. The return-to-duty test. It is a directly observed drug test, and the result must be negative. You can order it online once the SAP says you are eligible.
  5. Follow-up testing. This starts after you return to duty and continues under the SAP's plan.

After you return to duty

The SAP's follow-up testing plan requires at least six unannounced follow-up tests in the first 12 months, and the SAP can extend the plan up to 60 months. See our guide to the follow-up testing plan.

Where the Clearinghouse fits in

The SAP's evaluation steps, the negative return-to-duty result and completion of the follow-up testing plan are all recorded in the FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse. Your status stays "prohibited" until the required steps are recorded. For all six steps, read the return-to-duty process.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to return to duty after a positive test?

It depends on your SAP. You cannot do safety-sensitive work until the SAP evaluates you, you complete the education or treatment the SAP requires, the SAP says you are eligible, and you pass a return-to-duty test with a negative result.

Why is there no set number of weeks?

The SAP decides what education or treatment you need, and that varies from driver to driver. Federal rules set the steps, not a deadline.

Can I speed it up?

You can avoid delays: schedule the SAP evaluation promptly, finish the SAP's recommendations on time, and order your return-to-duty test as soon as the SAP says you are eligible.

Is it finished after the negative return-to-duty test?

No. You may return to duty after a negative result, but your SAP's follow-up testing plan continues. At least six unannounced follow-up tests are required in the first 12 months, and the plan can last up to 60 months.
