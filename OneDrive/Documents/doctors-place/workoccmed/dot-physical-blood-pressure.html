FMCSA Blood Pressure Standards

The 4 Blood Pressure Stages for CDL Drivers

Under 49 CFR Part 391.41, a commercial driver must not have a current clinical diagnosis of hypertension likely to interfere with the ability to operate a commercial vehicle safely. In practice, FMCSA uses four stages based on your reading on exam day.

Stage 3 means you cannot drive that day

A reading of 180/110 or higher is an immediate disqualification. You will need to get your blood pressure under control and return with documentation from your treating physician before a medical examiner can certify you.

Medications

Can You Have a CDL with High Blood Pressure Medicine?

Yes. FMCSA does not prohibit CDL drivers from taking antihypertensive medication. The standard is whether your blood pressure is controlled on the day of examination and whether the medication itself causes side effects that could impair safe driving.

Medications that are generally acceptable

✓ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril) — well-tolerated, no significant driving side effects
✓ARBs (losartan, valsartan) — generally acceptable, rare dizziness risk
✓Calcium channel blockers (amlodipine, diltiazem) — generally acceptable
✓Thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone) — generally acceptable
✓Beta-blockers (metoprolol, atenolol) — acceptable; examiner may note fatigue risk
ℹ️

Bring your medication list to the exam

Arrive with a complete list of your medications including dosages. If possible, bring a letter from your prescribing physician confirming your diagnosis and that your medication does not impair your ability to drive safely.

What the examiner evaluates

The medical examiner looks at three things: (1) your blood pressure reading on exam day, (2) the medication you are taking and its known side effects, and (3) whether you have any related conditions such as cardiovascular disease or kidney damage. If your BP is controlled and your medication is well-tolerated, most drivers pass without issue.

Exam Day Tips

How to Prepare for Your DOT Physical if You Have High Blood Pressure

1

Take your medication as prescribed

Do not skip your blood pressure medication before the exam. Take it on your normal schedule.

2

Avoid caffeine 2–3 hours before

Coffee, energy drinks, and some teas can temporarily raise blood pressure. Skip them the morning of your exam.

3

Arrive early and rest

Sit quietly for at least 5 minutes before your BP is taken. Walking in rushed or stressed will spike your reading.

4

Bring your medication list and physician letter

A letter from your treating doctor can help the examiner understand your treatment history and current control level.

5

Ask to rest and recheck if your reading is high

If your first reading is in Stage 3 territory, ask to sit quietly for 10–15 minutes and be retested. Many examiners will allow one recheck before recording a disqualifying result.
